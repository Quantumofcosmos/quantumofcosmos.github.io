On my reading list. Thaler's book is the most modern treatment of zero-knowledge proofs, covering SNARKs, STARKs, and the theoretical foundations. I'm particularly interested in the practical implications for blockchain privacy and verifiable computation.

## Why I Want to Read This

Zero-knowledge proofs are one of the most powerful tools in cryptography, and they're becoming increasingly practical. Thaler's book covers the modern landscape:

- **Interactive proofs**: The theoretical foundation
- **Argument systems**: Computationally sound proofs that are more efficient
- **SNARKs**: Succinct non-interactive arguments of knowledge, used in Zcash and Ethereum
- **STARKs**: Transparent setup, post-quantum secure, but larger proof sizes

The connection between theoretical computer science (IP = PSPACE, MIP = NEXP) and practical systems (Zcash, rollups) is fascinating. Thaler is uniquely positioned to cover both, as both a theorist and a practitioner.

## What I Hope to Learn

1. How to design and implement a simple SNARK system
2. The trade-offs between different proof systems (trusted setup vs. transparent, proof size vs. verification time)
3. The practical applications beyond cryptocurrency (verifiable computation, privacy-preserving analytics)
